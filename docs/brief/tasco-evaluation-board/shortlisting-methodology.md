# Use-Case-Based Proposal Review and Shortlisting

Developer handoff · 20 October 2026

## Objective

Review every submission against its selected enterprise use case, assess the supporting evidence, and recommend a shortlist tier with clear reasons. Support the operator's decision without automatically changing selections.

This is the **shortlisting methodology**, separate from the weighted Demo Day judging scorecard. Do not introduce automatic numerical weights for this stage.

## 1. Collect the complete submission

Review all relevant records:

- Program applications **and direct use-case proposals**, including unpublished records accessible to the operator.
- Application answers, solution profiles, company/team information and attached decks/proposals.
- Every PDF page, including screenshots, diagrams, tables and image-only content.

**Important:** An incomplete program application must not hide a complete proposal submitted through another route—as happened with Overleap.

Link related records, preserve their individual statuses, and distinguish different products from the same company. Record submission versions and attachment coverage. Deduplicate identical attachments without treating repeated content as independent evidence.

## 2. Understand the enterprise use case

Extract the enterprise's:

- Priority problems, intended users and target workflows.
- Desired business/customer outcomes.
- Explicit constraints and requirements.
- Available data, channels, interfaces and resources.
- Open assumptions and dependencies.

Separate **enterprise requirements** from **applicant assumptions**. Do not invent mandatory integrations, budgets or timelines.

## 3. Classify alignment separately from supplier strength

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Direct Alignment** | Directly addresses the priority use case or a substantive component of it. |
| **Industry Alignment** | Relevant to the industry or adjacent operations, but not the priority use case. |
| **Technology Alignment** | Current product is outside the use case/industry, but relevant technical capabilities could support adaptation. |

A mature company can have weak use-case fit. An early team can have strong fit. If no credible connection exists, flag that explicitly rather than forcing a technology connection.

## 4. Evaluate six criteria

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Team** | Relevant key members, technical expertise, industry experience, achievements and prior delivery. Identify who would deliver the project. |
| **Traction** | Actual customers, active pilots or ongoing PoCs. Separate proposed-product adoption from adjacent products and company-wide customer totals. |
| **Enterprise readiness** | Case studies, deployment evidence, integrations, support, data handling, security and appropriate human oversight. |
| **Product** | Useful differentiation, working capabilities and evidence of effectiveness. Separate implemented features, simulations and roadmap items. |
| **Funding** | Funding, bootstrapping, revenue or sustainability information. Contextual—not an independent selection gate. |
| **Proposal quality** | A specific explanation of how the solution addresses the use case, including workflow, implementation, dependencies, delivery and evaluation. |

### Evidence distinctions

- **Same-use-case customers:** actual adoption of the relevant product/workflow.
- **Adjacent product customers:** adoption in another workflow or industry; explain the transfer required.
- **Active pilot/ongoing PoC:** an external participant, defined scope and work underway. Record paid/unpaid status where disclosed.
- **Prototype only:** internal tests, synthetic demonstrations or a hosted build without established external adoption.
- Pipeline, expressions of interest and proposed future pilots are not active traction.
- Consultancy-wide client totals and prior team projects can support capability, but do not automatically establish adoption of the proposed product.

## 5. Label evidence behind individual findings

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Documented** | Specific supporting material exists. This does not automatically mean independently verified. |
| **Claimed** | Applicant assertion without sufficient supporting evidence. |
| **Not disclosed** | Information is missing from the reviewed material. |
| **Contradictory** | Material statements conflict and require clarification. |

Every material finding should reference its **source section or attachment/page**. A criterion can contain findings with different labels.

Missing information must be flagged, not invented or treated as proof that a capability does not exist. Where external verification is performed, identify its source and date separately from the submitted evidence.

## 6. Apply the proposal gate and early-stage exception

### Proposal gate

A submission needs a **substantive, use-case-specific proposal**. Generic AI benefits, capability lists or standard project phases are insufficient. Do not infer AI authorship from writing style.

Look for a coherent explanation of:

- The selected problem, intended users and workflow.
- How the solution works, including AI, rules, data, integrations and human responsibilities.
- Current capabilities versus simulations, planned work and third-party dependencies.
- A credible delivery/test route and meaningful measures of success.

A concise proposal can pass. Page count and presentation polish are not substitutes for substance. Missing one commercial detail alone does not automatically fail a substantive proposal.

### Early-stage exception

Without product traction, a submission can still qualify for **To consider** when it has a substantive proposal and either:

- **Strong relevant team experience**, or
- **A demonstrable prototype with a credible delivery plan**.

Screenshots alone do not verify working functionality. If verification is pending, make the recommendation provisional and specify the required demonstration.

If neither route is supported, explain why the current submission lacks a credible execution case. Where a focused clarification could resolve an important gap in a promising submission, identify it rather than presenting missing data as proof of incapability.

## 7. Recommend one of four tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong Match** | Strong opportunity within its alignment category, supported by relevant deployment/customer evidence and a credible proposal. |
| **Potential Match** | Credible opportunity supported by pilots, adjacent deployments or substantive delivery evidence; important gaps remain. |
| **To consider** | Specific proposal with promising but early evidence, including candidates qualifying through the early-stage exception. |
| **Strong No** | Insufficient fit, non-substantive proposal or no credible capability/delivery route supported by the evidence. |

Use qualitative judgement. Display alignment beside the tier so a strong industry supplier is not mistaken for a direct use-case match.

Missing funding or pricing alone should not trigger rejection. Inaccessible or unreviewed material should trigger a coverage warning, not a fabricated assessment. No recommendation tier constitutes deployment approval.

## 8. Produce concise, auditable results

For each candidate, return:

- **Alignment + recommendation tier**.
- **Why consider:** one short sentence.
- **Key concern:** one material gap.
- **Next step:** specific validation required.
- Six-criteria findings, evidence labels and source references.
- Missing information, contradictions and review limitations.
- Provisional status where applicable.

Suggested overview:

| Applicant / solution | Alignment | Tier | Why consider | Key concern | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [Category] | [Tier] | [Short sentence] | [Material gap] | [Specific validation] |

Keep detailed supporting notes available separately. Aim for approximately 18 words or fewer in each quick-note field.

For source coverage, record which form sections and attachments were reviewed, page counts, duplicate content and any inaccessible/unreadable pages. Do not claim complete coverage when it is incomplete.

## 9. Preserve operator control

Store these separately:

1. **System recommendation**.
2. **Operator's shortlist decision**.
3. **Platform status for each submission record**.

Allow operator overrides without rewriting the evidence. Preserve previous versions and explain recommendation changes when submissions or evaluation rules change.

Do not automatically change platform decisions, judge scores, shortlist selections or outcome notifications. A decision saved against a use-case proposal must not be represented as a decision saved against a separate program application.

## Expected outcome

An explainable shortlist assistant with strong use-case understanding, complete source coverage, evidence-based recommendations and clear human control.
