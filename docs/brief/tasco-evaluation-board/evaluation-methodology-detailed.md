# Enterprise Solution Evaluation Methodology

**Owner:** Kai Yong  
**Version:** 1.0 — 16 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Evaluate enterprise challenge submissions consistently, using the enterprise briefing and the applicant's actual evidence. Support shortlisting for interviews or pilots while distinguishing potential from readiness to deploy.

## 1. Core principles

- Keep **use-case fit separate from supplier strength**. A mature supplier can have weak fit; an early team can have a highly relevant solution.
- Assess six areas: **team, traction, enterprise readiness, product, funding and proposal quality**.
- Attach **evidence-confidence labels to individual findings**, not just the overall application.
- Flag information that is missing, unclear or contradictory. Missing evidence does not prove that a capability or customer does not exist.
- Distinguish a working prototype, an active pilot, a production deployment and a proposed future adaptation.
- Evaluate from the enterprise's perspective: what problem will this solve, how will it work, what must the enterprise provide, and what would justify progressing?
- Use qualitative judgement. Do not invent numerical scores, weights, mandatory integrations or procurement requirements.
- Keep the reviewer's recommendation separate from the user's final shortlist and the platform's saved decisions.

## 2. Establish the enterprise brief first

Record the following before evaluating applicants:

| Briefing element | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Business problem | The specific friction and who experiences it. |
| Priority use cases | The workflows and outcomes the enterprise actually wants to improve. |
| Success measures | Business/customer outcomes, baseline and any required measurement window. |
| Constraints | Explicit requirements concerning customer messaging, data, channels, product rules, timing or budget. |
| Available resources | Data, interfaces, enterprise owners and access that are confirmed available. |
| Open assumptions | Dependencies or requirements that still need clarification. |

Separate **mandatory requirements**, **preferences** and **applicant assumptions**. A vendor's proposed integration or budget is not an enterprise commitment. Reuse the evaluation method across enterprises, but reset the use-case definition and constraints for each brief.

## 3. Alignment categories: assess fit independently

Assign one primary alignment category and explain the connection to the brief.

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Direct Alignment** | The proposed solution directly addresses a priority enterprise use case or a substantive component needed to deliver it. |
| **Industry Alignment** | The solution is relevant to the enterprise's industry or adjacent operations, but does not directly address the priority use case. |
| **Technology Alignment** | The current solution is outside the use case and industry, but its demonstrated AI/technology capabilities could support an adaptation. |

If no credible relationship exists, state **no credible alignment** and recommend Strong No; do not force a technology connection. These categories are not a ranking of supplier quality. A strong supplier in Industry Alignment may still be a poor choice for the immediate use case.

## 4. Evidence-confidence labels

Use one label for each material claim. A criterion may contain findings with different labels.

| Label | Meaning | How to report it |
|---|---|---|
| **Documented** | Specific supporting material is supplied: for example, a substantive case study, implementation record, technical artefact or detailed career history. | Identify what the material supports and its limits. Submission-supported does **not** mean independently verified. |
| **Claimed** | The applicant asserts the fact, but sufficient supporting detail is absent. | Use “reports” or “claims”; specify the evidence needed to substantiate it. |
| **Not disclosed** | The reviewed submission does not provide the information. | State the missing item explicitly. Do not translate absence into a negative factual claim. |
| **Contradictory** | Material statements within the submission conflict. | Cite both statements and explain what needs reconciliation. Do not assume dishonesty. |

Apply labels narrowly. A named customer in a sentence can still be **Claimed**; a detailed customer case can be **Documented**, while its reported outcome remains unverified. External checks, if performed, must be identified separately with their sources and dates.

## 5. The six evaluation criteria

| Criterion | Questions to assess | Important distinctions |
|---|---|---|
| **Team** | Are relevant key members identified? Do they have strong technical expertise, relevant industry experience, specific achievements or prior delivery history? Who would deliver this project? | Named roles and concrete achievements carry more weight than company size, institution names, titles or generic years of experience. Advisers are not automatically delivery staff. |
| **Traction** | Does the proposed product have customers, an active pilot or an ongoing PoC? What is the stage, scope and evidence? | Separate same-use-case adoption, adjacent product adoption, active pilots and prototype-only work. Pipeline, interest and proposed future pilots are not active traction. |
| **Enterprise readiness** | Is there a substantive case study or proof of enterprise deployment? Are integration, support, security, data handling and customer-facing controls credible for this project? | Prior deployment supports capability, but does not establish readiness for a new industry, language or workflow. Assess readiness proportionately to the proposed next step. |
| **Product** | What is the key differentiator? Is it useful for this use case? Which features actually work, and what evidence supports their value? | Separate implemented features from mockups and roadmap items. A technology list, patent filing or polished demo does not by itself establish differentiation or effectiveness. |
| **Funding** | Is funding, bootstrapping, revenue or other sustainability information disclosed? | Funding is contextual. Missing funding is not an automatic rejection. Revenue, project contracts, pipeline, grants and investment are different facts. |
| **Proposal quality** | Does the submission clearly explain how it will address the enterprise use case and demonstrate a credible route to delivery and evaluation? | Specific mechanics and assumptions matter more than page count, presentation polish or generic project phases. Do not infer AI authorship from writing style. |

### Traction: record the type explicitly

- **Same-use-case customers:** actual adoption of the relevant product/workflow.
- **Adjacent product customers:** adoption in another workflow or industry; explain the transfer required.
- **Active pilot/ongoing PoC:** an external participant, defined scope and work underway. Distinguish paid and unpaid where disclosed.
- **Prototype only:** internal tests, synthetic demonstrations, friend tests or a hosted build without established external adoption.
- **Not substantiated:** the submission does not establish any of the above clearly enough.

Consultancy-wide client totals and earlier team projects may support supplier capability. They do not automatically establish adoption of the proposed product. Avoid counting the same fact as product traction and proof of the new adaptation.

### Proposal quality: review from the enterprise perspective

Look for a coherent explanation of:

1. **Selected problem and workflow:** users, trigger, steps, outputs and expected business benefit.
2. **Solution mechanics:** how AI, rules, data, interfaces and people contribute to the workflow.
3. **Current versus proposed capability:** what exists, what is simulated, what must be built and what depends on third parties.
4. **Delivery route:** a bounded implementation/test plan, dependencies and responsibilities on both sides.
5. **Evaluation:** what would demonstrate success, using meaningful outcomes rather than activity alone.
6. **Enterprise implications:** relevant customer-message controls, data handling, fallback arrangements, effort and commercial assumptions.

A concise proposal can qualify. Merely naming the enterprise, listing AI benefits or providing a standard build/test schedule does not qualify. Missing price or one unresolved detail alone does not automatically fail an otherwise substantive proposal; record the gap and its effect on feasibility.

## 6. Early-stage exception

Lack of product traction does **not** automatically mean Strong No.

An early-stage submission can qualify for **To consider** when it has:

- A substantive, use-case-specific proposal; **and**
- Either **strong relevant team experience** or **a demonstrable prototype with a credible delivery plan**.

For the team route, identify the specific members, careers, prior technical delivery, research achievements or industry experience supporting the exception.

For the prototype route, identify the working core functionality and evidence demonstrating it. Label simulated integrations and synthetic data explicitly. Static mockups alone do not demonstrate a working prototype. If the demo cannot be accessed or tested, record that limit; do not claim it was validated.

In both routes, explain the remaining gaps and the next interview, demonstration or validation needed. This exception supports further consideration; it does not establish enterprise deployment readiness.

If traction is unsubstantiated and neither exception is supported by the reviewed evidence, recommend **Strong No for the current submission**. Where a specific, answerable clarification could resolve a material gap in an otherwise promising submission, record it explicitly rather than presenting missing data as proof of incapability.

## 7. Recommendation tiers

Apply the tier to the quality of the opportunity and supplier evidence, with the alignment category displayed separately.

| Tier | Decision meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong Match** | Strong fit within the stated alignment category, relevant customer/deployment evidence, credible capability and a substantive enterprise proposal. Remaining gaps do not undermine the case for progressing. State whether the fit is to the priority use case or only adjacent industry work. |
| **Potential Match** | A credible use-case opportunity supported by relevant pilots, adjacent product deployment or substantive delivery evidence, but important adaptation, validation or execution gaps remain. |
| **To consider** | A substantive proposal with a promising but early or weakly evidenced execution case. May qualify through the early-stage exception. Worth a targeted interview or demonstration. |
| **Strong No** | Insufficient credible fit, a generic/non-substantive proposal, or no credible capability/delivery route supported by the submission after considering traction and both early-stage exceptions. Explain the specific reason and what could change the decision. |

No tier is automatic deployment approval. Do not mechanically promote an applicant because it has funding, many customers or a detailed deck. Likewise, do not reject solely for missing funding, an unnamed customer under NDA or lack of insurance traction when credible adjacent capability exists.

## 8. Source review and PDF coverage

1. Capture the application ID, version, submitted date and platform status. Identify duplicates and revised submissions.
2. Read the complete application form, including team, traction and proposal answers.
3. Review every submitted attachment. For PDFs, read each page's text and inspect rendered pages for diagrams, screenshots, tables, footnotes and image-only content. Enlarge or extract difficult sections as needed.
4. Record each attachment's page count and actual review coverage. Identify exact duplicate content so it is not treated as independent corroboration.
5. Reconcile statements across forms, decks and proposals. Distinguish reported results, targets, illustrative calculations and proposed outcomes.
6. Keep source references for material findings: application section, attachment name and page number.
7. Flag unreadable, missing, inaccessible or unreviewed material. Do not claim a complete review when coverage is incomplete, or promise that no detail could have been missed.

Use the submission as the primary evaluation record. If external research or reference checks are added, keep their findings separate from what the applicant actually submitted.

## 9. Risk and shortlist decisions

Separate three questions:

- **Worth considering?** Fit, differentiation, team, evidence and proposal.
- **Ready for a pilot?** Bounded scope, responsibilities, access, costs and appropriate controls.
- **Ready for deployment?** Demonstrated performance, accepted controls and approved operating arrangements.

Distinguish weak evidence from demonstrated misconduct or actual harm. State the potential failure and the evidence behind it. Material contradictions, misleading success definitions or unresolved customer/data risks can justify a hold until resolved; do not automatically label the supplier harmful.

The user's shortlist is a separate decision layer. Preserve it even when the assessment differs. Do not change platform decisions, judge scores or shortlist selections without authorization.

## 10. Output format: concise first, evidence available

Use a quick overview table:

| Applicant | Alignment | Tier | Why consider | Key concern | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [Category] | [Tier] | [One short sentence] | [One material gap] | [Specific validation] |

Aim for no more than about 18 words in each quick-note field. Avoid dense paragraphs in the overview.

## 11. Updating an existing evaluation

- Identify new applications and revised versions before reevaluating.
- Preserve earlier evaluations and explain material tier changes.
- When applying a new filter, state the targeted group and leave other entries unchanged unless a broader review is requested.
- Keep an audit trail of evidence, confidence labels, rationale and date.
- Refresh counts and summary views consistently, while preserving the user's shortlist as a separate layer.

## 12. Methodology boundary

This guide incorporates the agreed evidence-confidence labels, broader early-stage exception and separation of alignment from supplier strength. It retains the six criteria and four tiers. It does not introduce numerical weighting or an automatic rejection rule for missing commercial or funding details.
