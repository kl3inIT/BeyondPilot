# Landing page

Written on 3 October 2026. How the home page got its shape: the structures considered, the two design critiques, and where each image on the page comes from. The page as designed is described in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

## Who the page serves

- AI providers and builders, often on a phone from a campaign link and facing a deadline: apply to an open program.
- Enterprise innovation teams: publish a use case, or talk to GenAI Fund.
- People browsing: explore solutions, use cases and talent.

What they must come to believe: GenAI Fund's programs are real routes to a pilot with a real enterprise, because the people who set a brief hold the budget and sit at demo day; and applying is light, with no finished product needed and one profile to reuse.

The proof on hand is real: the Tasco challenge with its dates, judges and investment of up to US$100,000; 21 programs and 56 events; partner logos; founders in GenAI Fund's network; photographs of the programs. BeyondPilot has no usage numbers or testimonials of its own yet, and none are invented.

## Structures considered

Ranked by how well each fits the audience and the proof:

1. **Two doors.** After the hero, two journeys side by side: "I have an AI solution" and "I have a business problem".
2. **Intent router.** The hero asks what brings the visitor; the answer reorders the page.
3. **Campaign first.** The open campaign is the hero, with its countdown, question and judges.
4. **Program timeline.** A vertical timeline is the spine: open now, coming up, done.
5. **Pilot pipeline.** The cycle from use case to pilot as stations filled with real items.
6. **Directory as the page.** The hero's search opens straight into a filterable directory.
7. **Proof wall.** A mosaic of real programs, partners, founders and photographs.

The page that was approved combines three of them: a hero with one search over everything and real cards floating around it (6), the program timeline as the first section (4), and a tabbed preview of the directory. The two-door layout was dropped: a pair of audience boxes reads as a template.

## Critiques

Both runs used the Impeccable critique, scored on Nielsen's heuristics out of 36.

| Run                        | Score | Main issues                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First                      | 20/36 | Rows cut at the phone's edge and floating cards overlapping on a phone; two primary actions in one view; an azure too light for text; purple inside posters and logos |
| Second, after the redesign | 25/36 | See below                                                                                                                                                             |

Open issues after the second run, in priority order:

1. The AI solutions tab is a logo directory: no proof line and no link to a solution.
2. Use case covers are event photographs unrelated to the use case.
3. Touch targets on a phone are under 44px: the large button is 40px, scope chips 26px, arrow links 20px. Fixed in code with a 44px hit area on every small control.
4. The hero's subtitle has a contrast of 3.8–4.0:1 over the glow.
5. On a phone the path to the deadline is weak: no time on the chip, Apply far down the page, no "Open now" row in the menu.

Smaller points: card titles at three sizes; the "Open now" pill looks like a primary button; the programs tab repeats the timeline; shadows and the 40px headings had no shared style in Figma.

## Rules learned

- Real content only: a card, a number or a name on the page exists outside the page.
- Nothing is cut at the edge on a phone; a row that would scroll sideways becomes a stack or a grid.
- One primary action per view: on the home page it is Search, so "Get started" in the header is secondary.
- A recurring event is one card with a tile per date.
- No purple or indigo, including inside photographs, posters and logos: crop, compose or choose another image.
- A photograph is checked for what it shows before it is used. One showed a venue's Wi-Fi password on a whiteboard and was cropped.
- Small frames hide defects: a phone frame is checked section by section at full size.

The network section was studied on Mobbin on 4 October 2026. Hub-and-spoke layouts (Apollo, Front) belong to software integrations with small square icons and were set aside, as was the wall of floating tiles (Mistral AI, Lattice), which drops the roles and repeats the hero.
