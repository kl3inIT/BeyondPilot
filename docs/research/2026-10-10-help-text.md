# Help text: what is shown, what is behind a click, what is removed

Read on 10 October 2026, to decide what to do about the explanatory text of the application. The owner of the product found it carries too many subtitles and proposed moving them into tooltips or behind a "?". This note records what the interface holds, what the sources say, and the rules the team adopted from them, which are in [conventions › Help text](../conventions.md#help-text).

## What the interface holds

Counted in `web/messages/en.json` on 10 October 2026, of about 4,900 strings:

| Kind                                                 | Strings |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| `lead`: the line under a title                       | 178     |
| Keys ending in `Hint`, and `hint`                    | 122     |
| `description`                                        | 129     |
| `note`                                               | 39      |
| `what`: the line beside a section of a settings page | 18      |

The longest `lead` is 277 characters; many are 160 to 180. In the source, `FieldDescription` is used 65 times, `Tooltip` 4 times and `Popover` 3 times.

## What the design critique of the same day showed

The critique of 10 October measured the application on staging. Three of its findings bear on where help text lives:

- The description of a judging criterion is a `title` attribute, which touch and keyboard never reach; the full time of an audit event is in a hover tooltip, absent on a phone.
- On the matching page the meaning of each bucket is behind an (i) button, and the critique scored that as a fault: a reader does not know what a bucket means without opening each.
- Text that is shown was named as a strength where it says a consequence or who reads something: the email setup checklist, "Only GenAI Fund reads this" beside a judge's note, the consequence in a confirmation.

So moving every explanation behind a tooltip would repair one fault by spreading another. Help text is sorted by what it is for.

## Sources

| Source                                                                                                                                          | What it says                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nielsen Norman Group, [Tooltip Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tooltip-guidelines/), Alita Kendrick, 27 January 2019               | "Don't use tooltips for information that is vital to task completion." "Users shouldn't need to find a tooltip in order to complete their task." Tooltips "are not normally available on touchscreens". "Provide Tooltips for Unlabeled Icons."                         |
| GOV.UK Design System, [Text input](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/)                                                 | "Use hint text for help that's relevant to the majority of users." "Keep hint text to a single short sentence, without any full stops." "Do not use links in hint text." Placeholder text is not used for hints or examples: "it vanishes when the user starts typing". |
| GOV.UK Design System, [Details](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/)                                                       | "Use the details component to make a page easier to scan when it contains information that only some users will need." "Do not use the details component to hide information that the majority of your users will need."                                                |
| W3C, [Understanding SC 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/labels-or-instructions.html) (Level A)         | "Labels or instructions are provided when content requires user input." The intent is "not to clutter the page with unnecessary information"; "Too much information or instruction can be just as harmful as too little."                                               |
| W3C, [Understanding SC 1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html) (Level AA) | Content that appears on hover or focus must be dismissible, hoverable and persistent.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Impeccable skill, `reference/clarify.md`                                                                                                        | "Helper text answers an implicit question instead of restating the control. Use progressive disclosure for uncommon detail."                                                                                                                                            |

The method these describe together is progressive disclosure: show what is needed now and let the rest be opened. None of the sources gives a table that sorts a given sentence; where "most people" ends and "some people" begins is judged line by line.

## What was adopted

Seven rules, each from the sources above:

| Rule                                                                          | Rests on                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text that repeats its label or title is removed                               | SC 3.3.2 (intent); `clarify.md`                           |
| What most people need to act correctly is shown where they act                | GOV.UK hint text; SC 3.3.2                                |
| Help text that is shown is one short sentence without a link                  | GOV.UK hint text                                          |
| What an action leads to is shown beside the action                            | NN/g, guideline 1                                         |
| What only some people need is behind a control that opens on a click or a tap | GOV.UK Details                                            |
| A hover tooltip holds no information; it names an icon-only control           | NN/g, guidelines 1 and 3 and "unlabeled icons"; SC 1.4.13 |
| A placeholder is not a hint or an example                                     | GOV.UK text input                                         |

## What was not adopted

- Moving every subtitle into a tooltip, as first proposed: a hover tooltip does not open on a phone, where most applicants are.
- GOV.UK's "without any full stops" for hint text: the application's voice writes full sentences, and its hints end with a full stop.

## How the text is reviewed

One group of pages at a time. Every line of help text of the group is listed with what is proposed for it (remove, keep and shorten, or move behind a click), the rule that applies and the new wording; nothing is removed or rewritten before the list is approved.
