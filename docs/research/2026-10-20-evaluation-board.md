# Evaluation board: who has built an AI shortlist assessment, and what the repository already has

Research date: 20 October 2026, for BEY-234. The board follows Kai's methodology in [docs/brief/tasco-evaluation-board/](../brief/tasco-evaluation-board/shortlisting-methodology.md): per candidate, an alignment, six criteria whose findings carry an evidence label and a source, a proposal gate with an early-stage exception, and one of four tiers, kept apart from the operator's decision. **[V]** marks what was read in the repository or the official documentation; **[I]** what was inferred or taken from marketing and third-party pages. Repositories read were cloned to the git-ignored `.tmp/shortlist-refs/`.

## Summary

- Nothing found, open source or commercial, implements the whole methodology. Nobody combines a proposal gate with an early-stage exception, alignment kept apart from supplier strength, traction typed by kind, tiers without weights, and the system's recommendation, the operator's decision and the platform status as three separate records.
- The pieces exist and are spread across about eight sources: findings with a page and an exact quote, labels for missing and contradictory evidence, a question to send back to the applicant, a quote check that also compares numbers, runs compared finding by finding, and evaluations stamped with the model, prompt and rubric versions.
- About half of what the board needs is already in the repository, in matching (BEY-39, BEY-229): runs, reading PDFs including image-only pages, the quote check and the fingerprint that avoids asking twice.

## Wording of evidence labels

- **AVeriTeC (FEVER 2024)** **[V]**: Supported, Refuted, Not Enough Evidence, Conflicting Evidence/Cherry-picking; a verdict counts only when both the label and the evidence are right. It has no level for "the applicant asserts it without proof", which Kai's Claimed adds.
- **Admiralty code** **[V]**: grades the reliability of the source and the credibility of the information on separate axes, which supports keeping "who says it" (the form's self-report against a customer letter in the deck) apart from "is it supported".
- **GRADE** **[I]**: certainty downgraded for indirectness, a close analogue to Industry and Technology alignment against Direct.

## The model and PDFs

- **Anthropic PDF support and Citations** **[V]** ([PDF](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support), [Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)): a page is sent as its image and its text; citations return a page range; "Citations and structured outputs are incompatible" (a 400), and scanned or image-only pages cannot be cited. The source of a finding therefore stays a page and a quote the model writes in its structured answer and code checks, as matching does.
- **Spring AI 2.0** **[V]** ([LLM as judge](https://docs.spring.io/spring-ai/reference/guides/llm-as-judge.html)): a rating record through `.entity(...)` and a separate judge model; the built-in evaluators answer pass or fail only; no Java example with citations.
- **LLM grant review, arXiv 2603.08281** **[V, abstract]**: reviewing section by section beat a single pass and a council of personas; alignment flaws were caught reliably and clarity flaws mostly missed, so Proposal quality is the criterion least safe to automate.
- **SSFF, arXiv 2405.19456** **[V, abstract]**: plain LLMs systematically over-predict startup success; the model should state the evidence before the tier, and the share of Strong No is worth watching.

## What the repository already has

- **Matching runs** (`matching`, [BEY-229 design](../increments/active/bey-229-proposal-evaluation/design.md), [ADR 0010](../decisions/0010-matching-reads-the-proposals-a-use-case-received.md)): a scheduled worker that waits `settleMinutes` after a change, judges candidates and the proposals a use case received, keeps steps and cost per run, retries a refusal; `Asking` (Spring AI `ChatClient`, `BeanOutputConverter`, one retry), prompts as constants with versions in `Prompts`, a SHA-256 fingerprint over prompt versions and material so unchanged material is not asked again.
- **Reading material**: `ProposalSources` labels each text (`proposal`, `team`, `proposal deck p.3`, `solution 1 deck p.2`); `shared.PdfPages` and `ai.DocumentPages` read image-only pages by a vision model or OCR; a PDF that gives no text is listed as unread.
- **Quote check**: `Quotes` classifies a quote as exact, from another source, close, not found or none; a quote not found lowers its finding, and code never raises one.
- **Program applications**: `proposal.answers` and `proposal_version.snapshot` per submission (answers with labels, files, the deck); decisions in `proposal.review_status` and `proposal_review_decision`. No AI reads them yet, and an application names no use case.
- **Use-case proposals**: `use_case_proposal`, edited in place, with no version history; the organization's decision in `decision` and `use_case_proposal_decision`.
- **Operator MCP** (BEY-222): `get_application` and `read_application_file` read an application in full; `get_proposal` does not read a proposal's PDF.

## Kai's mockup data

The mockup's data (kept out of the repository; it holds real submissions) has 48 evaluations made by Kai between 16 and 19 October from the live platform. Labels over 288 main findings: 174 Claimed, 66 Documented, 47 Not disclosed, 1 Contradictory; 18 extra findings under a criterion; 39 evaluations marked as needing a refresh after the methodology changed; one candidate links two records (a program application and a use-case proposal). The production board is assessed by the platform's own model; the mockup data is only a comparison for the first runs.
