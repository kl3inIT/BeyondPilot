# Review and judging of applications

Written on 6 October 2026, before planning the review of applications ([BEY-38](https://linear.app/beyondpilot/issue/BEY-38)). The review screens O1–O3 had been drawn from hiring tools (Pin, Wrangle, Workable, Homerun, Wellfound; [flows and references, G](2026-10-02-flows-and-references.md)), which shortlist candidates but do not judge a competition. This note reads how competitions and award programs judge, and how product screens score, invite and release, to check those screens.

Two kinds of source:

- **How judging is run**: the public help centers and rules of Devpost, Devpost for Teams, Submittable, Submittable Next, Award Force, Evalato, Y Combinator, Gust, an EU open call on F6S, Airtable, Agentic AI Build Week 2026 and AngelHack. Every claim is cited in [sources/2026-10-06-judging-platforms.md](sources/2026-10-06-judging-platforms.md). No account was created and nothing was submitted.
- **How the screens look**: Mobbin, web.

The brief leaves the details open: an invited reviewer or judge "reviews assigned applications … specific access and scoring requirements need confirmation", and "scoring criteria, reviewer roles and outcome-release controls will be agreed during discovery" ([brief, 7.7](../brief/BeyondPilot-Vendor-Product-Brief-and-Scope.md)).

## What judging platforms agree on

1. **A judge sees only the entries given to them.** Seeing everything is an organizer's role. Entries are given by category, panel, round or stage; Submittable Next and Award Force can also give every judge every entry.
2. **An external judge is invited by email and signs in to a personal account**: a private link and an account (Devpost), a password (Submittable), or a six-digit email code (Award Force). None documents a link that works without an account.
3. **Several criteria, each on a small scale, averaged across judges.** Devpost scores 1–5 stars per criterion; AngelHack 0–5 on five criteria; Agentic AI Build Week, which GenAI Fund ran, judged six criteria (agentic AI use, problem and track fit, technical execution, impact, creativity, pitch). Weights are optional: absent on Devpost.com, percentages on Devpost for Teams, Submittable Next and Evalato.
4. **Judges do not see each other's scores by default.** Award Force and Evalato can turn it on for a round; vendors frame hiding them as protection against bias.
5. **A judge can step back from an entry** (recuse, abstain, conflict of interest), the organizer can remove a judge, and a declined score is left out of the average.
6. **The organizer follows each judge's progress**, as entries scored of entries given.
7. **A person decides; the scores only order the list.** Devpost, Submittable Next and Award Force have the organizer pick from a list sorted by the average; only Evalato and AngelHack describe a computed winner.
8. **Scores and decisions are separate records**, and entrants never see an individual judge's score. When feedback is shared, the organizer chooses to, anonymised, usually as an average with selected comments.
9. **The submission is usually frozen at the deadline.** Award Force is the exception: entrants edit until the round closes and judges see the live entry; Submittable Next tags every later edit.
10. **Release is one act and is rarely undone.** Devpost announces now or on a schedule; Submittable Next tells applicants nothing until offers go out, and closing a cycle sends the "Not Selected" emails and cannot be undone.

Where they differ: whether a saved score can be edited (always, until judging ends, or locked a few minutes after saving), and whether comments are required.

The Mobbin screens add two details to the review panel: a note says who reads it ("Only GenAI Fund and its judges see this"), and the panel steps through the applications with a Skip.
